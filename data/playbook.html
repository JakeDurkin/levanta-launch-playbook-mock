Marketplace Integration Launch Playbook

Version 1.0, drafted Oct 16, 2026 from the Wayfair launch (May to Oct 2026). Built for the next marketplace: Target, Best Buy, Chewy or whoever signs next.

How to use this playbook

  • One place. Everything a launch needs is written here: phases, gates, owners, checklists, templates and the tracker setup. You should not need to open another doc to run a launch.
  • One tracker. Work is tracked in a Linear project cloned from the template in Section 8. Slack is for talking. Drive is for files. Nothing else.
  • L = go-live day. All timing is in weeks before or after the day suppliers can connect (Wayfair: Oct 5, 2026). Marketing launch can follow up to a week later, as it did for Wayfair (Oct 13).
  • Labels. [C] means confirmed from Slack, email, Drive, Linear, Notion or meeting notes. [I] means inferred and should be checked. <Role> means the owner is not named yet.

The short version

  • A new marketplace moves through 7 phases. Each phase ends with a hard gate that the Launch PM closes in Linear.
  • Before kickoff, Exec and Legal fill a one-page Commercial Terms Sheet. Most Wayfair pain (CSM coverage, the GMV definition, how the managed account pays) came from questions this sheet now answers up front.
  • One named Launch PM owns the whole thing end to end. On Wayfair that was Jake Durkin. For the next marketplace the proposed owner is Stacey (likely default PM, pending Jake's sign-off), confirmed at kickoff.
  • Every launch gets the same four Slack channels, the same Drive folder layout and the same 24-piece asset kit.
  • Go/No-Go is a scored checklist, not a feeling. Hypercare lasts 6 weeks with daily triage and a 48-hour SLA on partner asks.

2. Phases and gates

Each phase lists its timing, the accountable owner, what must be true to start, the work, the exit gate, and what it looked like for Wayfair.

Phase 1. Deal, SOW and Kickoff

Timing: L-24 to L-18 weeks

Accountable: Exec sponsor (Wayfair: Rob Schab) until signature, then the Launch PM (Wayfair: Jake Durkin. Next launch: Stacey (likely default PM, pending Jake's sign-off)).

Entry: A partner has verbally committed and a draft SOW exists.

Work:

  • Negotiate the MSA and SOW. Price the build in milestones that match how the build really runs.
  • Fill the Commercial Terms Sheet (template in Section 6). It has to answer: Tracked GMV definition, CSM coverage, how partner-managed accounts pay, acceptance criteria, escrow and attribution windows, terminology, marketing rules.
  • Name the Launch PM and every role in Section 4. Name a backup for each partner POC.
  • Clone the Linear template. Open the four Slack channels. Create the Drive folder.
  • Partner vendor setup (Wayfair used Coupa with two POs). Create the Stripe customer for build and SaaS invoicing.
  • Run the supplier and creator overlap analysis (Wayfair: 134 strong supplier matches).
  • Hold kickoff with the partner and agree the cadence in Section 7.

Exit gate: G1 Kickoff Ready: SOW signed, terms sheet complete and signed off by Exec, Legal and Finance, every role named, Linear and channels live, first partner sync held.

Wayfair: SOW signed May 19 and 20, 2026 [C]. Linear project created May 21 from Rob's Excel task list [C]. The terms sheet questions were answered later and piecemeal: CSM coverage on Sep 30 and Oct 6, managed-account payment on Oct 9, the GMV definition still open on Oct 15 [C].

Phase 2. Technical Integration

Timing: L-18 to L-6 weeks

Accountable: Product lead (Wayfair: Neehar Swali).

Entry: G1 closed. Sandbox or API access requested.

Work:

  • Discovery and tech requirements. Pick the product ID (Wayfair: Supplier Part Number).
  • Build catalog sync, order or conversion data, attribution and billing. For Wayfair that meant the Supplier API (later an SFTP feed plus a daily supplemental feed), the CJ Affiliate API and the CJ Advertiser API [C].
  • Sign any network or data-sharing contracts (Wayfair: CJ publisher agreement and CJ data sharing agreement, signed Aug 12) [C].
  • Log every scope change as a change order in Linear the day it comes up (white-label brands came up Jul 14) [C].
  • Check billing milestones against actual progress each month.
  • Get the first attributed conversion in production.

Exit gate: G2 Build Accepted: a real production conversion is attributed end to end, and the partner confirms acceptance in writing. The Wayfair MSA says nothing counts as accepted until Wayfair confirms in writing [C].

Wayfair: First attributed conversion Aug 5 [C]. The go-live slipped from early September to Oct 5 because of partner-side feed and tracking changes [C]. No written acceptance record turned up in Slack or email [I, confirm with Rob or Neehar].

Phase 3. Product Readiness and UAT

Timing: L-8 to L-2 weeks

Accountable: Product lead, with the CS lead for test suppliers.

Entry: Core build in staging. Test supplier candidates named by the partner.

Work:

  • Configure the marketplace plan: what suppliers pay and what they see. Hide Levanta fees on partner-funded accounts.
  • Payments: card or Stripe for self-serve, and a separate invoice or PO path for partner-managed accounts.
  • CSV product upload with a real example file. Logos and brand handling. Bulk activation tools.
  • Signup path (Wayfair: /auth/wayfair with Wayfair login). Supplier terms shown at activation.
  • HubSpot properties and a Hex onboarding dashboard. A "first brand" Slack alert.
  • UAT with at least 3 test suppliers who go all the way through payment and product activation.
  • Write the launch-day runbook: feature flags, cron schedules, logs to watch.

Exit gate: G3 Product Ready: 3 or more test suppliers completed signup, payment and product activation without engineering workarounds. Fee display verified on every account type. Dashboards show real data.

Wayfair: Beta stalled on Stripe for test suppliers Everrest and Live Comfortably [C]. "Levanta Fees" and a 3.5% fee showed on Wayfair accounts after launch [C]. The managed account could not pay by Stripe, and Neehar bypassed the restriction on Oct 9 [C].

Phase 4. Assets and Enablement

Timing: L-10 to L-1 weeks

Accountable: GTM Enablement lead (Wayfair: Jake Durkin).

Entry: Program name, terminology and positioning agreed with the partner.

Work:

  • Build the 24-piece asset kit in Section 6. Get partner approval in writing.
  • KB articles in every launch language. Wayfair needed English and Mandarin [C].
  • Internal training by persona (Sales, CS, Support, Creator), each covering why, who, how and objections. Do not schedule it during sales month-end close.
  • Partner training for their reps (Wayfair trained SAMs on Oct 1) [C].
  • APAC training in local language, timed around regional holidays.
  • Freeze the UI before recording screenshots and demos.

Exit gate: G4 Enablement Ready: partner has approved the kit, KB is live in all launch languages, and every internal team has been trained with attendance logged.

Wayfair: The Sep 29 internal enablement drew feedback from Jamie: no clear "why", unclear audience, nothing for creator or CS teams, no objection handling, and it landed at sales month-end [C]. The demo video got 7 views and only 1 viewer reached the payments step, so it was split into chapters on Oct 6 [C].

Phase 5. Supplier Recruitment and Launch

Timing: L-4 to L+1 weeks

Accountable: Launch PM.

Entry: G3 and G4 closed, or on track with a dated plan.

Work:

  • Get the partner's ranked supplier list. Agree in writing which tiers get a dedicated CSM.
  • Assign CSMs to the top tier before launch day. Set up the handoff tracker.
  • Connect priority creators and publishers ahead of time.
  • Run the readiness scorecard (Section 10) and hold Go/No-Go.
  • Soft launch: flip the flag and watch the feeds.
  • Marketing launch: the partner leads the announcement and Levanta follows the same day.

Exit gate: G5 Go/No-Go: scorecard 85 or higher with no red must-haves. Both launch dates are clear of partner code freezes, regional holidays and month-end.

Wayfair: Connection opened to all suppliers Oct 5. Official marketing launch Oct 13 at 8am ET, with Wayfair leading [C]. The APAC holiday ran Oct 1 to 7, right over the soft launch [C].

Phase 6. Hypercare

Timing: L to L+6 weeks

Accountable: Launch PM, with the CS lead.

Entry: Launch has happened.

Work:

  • Daily 15-minute triage through L+2 weeks, then twice a week.
  • New-brands rank flow: every first brand gets ranked against the partner priority list and routed to a CSM within 1 business day.
  • Partner follow-up list: every ask becomes a Linear issue within 24 hours and gets a reply within 48 hours.
  • Data reconciliation against partner data (orders, prices, fees).
  • First invoice cycle: supplier invoices, creator payouts and the partner's own invoices.
  • Weekly working session with the partner.

Exit gate: G6 Hypercare Exit: 2 straight weeks with no Sev1 or Sev2, partner follow-up list closed or scheduled, and activation on pace against target.

Wayfair: Wayfair hypercare is live now. See Section 13 for Emily's Oct 14 list, the order-feed gap of 10 to 13%, CJ price corrections blocked by Wayfair's code freeze, and the open Linear issues [C].

Phase 7. Steady State

Timing: L+6 weeks onward

Accountable: Account owner (Wayfair: Stacey Georgoulis, who also owns the GMV target) [C].

Entry: G6 closed.

Work:

  • Monthly Tracked GMV report with the method written out (the Wayfair SOW requires one) [C].
  • Quarterly SaaS invoice in advance and variable fee in arrears.
  • QBRs with top suppliers. Monthly business review with the partner.
  • Roadmap items that came out of hypercare.
  • Retro. Update this playbook within 2 weeks.

Exit gate: G7 Running: first monthly KPI report delivered and accepted, invoicing on schedule, retro done and the playbook updated.

Wayfair: Wayfair's KPI is at least $7.5M Tracked GMV a month for 2 straight months in each 12-month period, with the first period running months 7 to 18 after commencement [C].

A1. Commercial Terms Sheet

Fill this before kickoff. Exec, Legal and Finance sign it off. Every line is something that cost Wayfair time later.

Field

Answer

Marketplace, sites in scope (Wayfair: wayfair.com only, not AllModern, Joss & Main or Birch Lane)

Signers and acceptance signer on each side

Build fee and milestones (with dates and acceptance criteria per milestone)

SaaS fee, billing frequency, first invoice trigger

Variable fee rate and the exact Tracked GMV definition (Levanta-attributed only, or all network sales). Give 2 worked examples.

KPI target, measurement window, reporting cadence and method

How the partner pays (portal, PO numbers, invoice address)

How partner-managed supplier accounts pay commission (invoice, PO or card)

What suppliers see and pay (fees hidden or shown)

CSM coverage: how many suppliers get a dedicated CSM, and by what rank

Unclaimed or off-network creator handling and escrow window

Attribution window and lock or returns window

Program name and terminology

Marketing rules and press approval path

POCs and backups on both sides

Data or network agreements needed

Languages and regions (APAC?)

Known blackout dates: partner code freezes, regional holidays, month-end

A3. Supplier one-pager

  • Headline: the outcome for the supplier, in their words (Wayfair: "affiliate" and "supplier").
  • Three benefits, one line each.
  • How it works in 3 steps: sign in with your <marketplace> account, add payment, activate products.
  • What it costs: commission to affiliates only, plus any fee rules from the terms sheet.
  • Proof: one number or quote.
  • Call to action: landing page link and a support contact.
  • Disclaimer line (TJ asked for one on v3).
  • Localized version: translate, then have a native speaker on the APAC team review it.

A8. Demo script outline

Keep each chapter under 3 minutes. Put Payments early. On Wayfair only 1 of 7 viewers reached the payments step when it came last.

  1. Sign up with the marketplace login (about 1 min).
  2. Add payment method (about 2 min). Explain how invoicing works: one monthly invoice.
  3. Connect the marketplace account and brands (about 2 min).
  4. Brand profile and product activation, including the CSV path (about 3 min).
  5. Set commissions with category guidance (about 2 min).
  6. Find and approve creators, including unclaimed ones (about 2 min).
  7. Reporting: where conversions show and how long data takes (Wayfair: 24 to 48 hours) (about 2 min).

8. Single source of truth: Linear

The recommendation

Use one Linear project per marketplace, cloned from a template. Engineering already lives in Linear: the Wayfair Integration project has 174 issues and milestones that mirror the SOW [C]. The gap was everyone else. GTM, KB and website work sat in Asana, engineering release and test plans sat in Notion, and the partner ran its own sheets [C]. Bringing the non-engineering work into the same project is the smallest change that gives one view of the whole launch.

Project setup

  • Name: Marketplace Launch: <Name>. Lead: the Launch PM. Target date: L.
  • Milestones: P1 Deal & Kickoff, P2 Technical Integration, P3 Product Readiness, P4 Assets & Enablement, P5 Recruitment & Launch, P6 Hypercare, P7 Steady State.
  • Lane labels: lane:pm, lane:exec, lane:legal-finance, lane:product, lane:eng, lane:gtm, lane:marketing, lane:cs, lane:apac, lane:support-kb, lane:creator, lane:revops, lane:partner.
